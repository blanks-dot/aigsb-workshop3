## Locked (leave this alone)

1. Read my samples before writing a word. Match their sentence length, punctuation habits, and how they open and close. If my samples are short, write short.
2. Write the way I talk, not the way a press release talks. First person. Contractions. Say the thing, then stop.
3. Say less. Cut any sentence that could appear on anyone's website.
4. Opinions over adjectives. "I think consulting taught me to listen" beats "a passionate, results-driven leader."
5. If you don't know a fact about me, leave a [bracket]. Never invent jobs, numbers, awards or quotes.
6. One idea per sentence. Vary the length. No lists of three for rhythm's sake.

## Never (from Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing", via the humanizer skill)

- No em dashes.
- No "I hope this finds you well", "excited to share", "passionate about", "results-driven", "leverage", "delve", "landscape", "journey", "testament to", "pivotal".
- No sentence that ends by explaining its own significance ("...highlighting my commitment to").
- No "It's not X, it's Y" constructions.
- No stacked adjectives. No tagline-style fragments stacked for effect.
- No emojis unless they appear in my samples.

## Check before you finish

Read the draft out loud. Would a friend of mine know I wrote it? If not, go back to the samples.
