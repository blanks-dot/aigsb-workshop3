07

Left alone, AI drifts to the average

Key takeaway: Without precision, AI goes back to the average.

  • Researchers played telephone with AI. One model draws a picture from a sentence, another describes the picture, and they repeat that 100 times.
  • 100 different starting sentences, 700 runs, and they ended up as just 12 kinds of picture: stormy lighthouses, cities at night, palace interiors.
  • Each round drops what's unusual and fills the gap with the most common version. They called it visual elevator music. (Hintze et al., Patterns, 2025)
  • Your bio and your site drift the same way unless you steer.
08

People trust work less when they think AI made it

Key takeaway: Average AI output costs you trust. People read it as low effort.

  • 13 experiments, 5,000+ people. The work was identical. The only change was whether people were told AI helped.
  • Trust dropped in all 13. In these three it fell 16 to 20%: students judging a professor, investors judging a fund, clients judging a designer. (Schilke and Reimann, OBHDP, 2025)
  • "Slop" was Merriam-Webster's 2025 word of the year: low-quality content made in bulk by AI. It has become the word for low-effort AI content.
  • Hiding AI isn't the answer. Make the output clearly yours.
09

A skill is a recipe AI reads before it cooks

Key takeaway: We want precision without spending a million years directing every prompt. A skill does the directing once.

  • A skill is a recipe card. Claude can cook, it just doesn't know your family's recipe.
  • One voice skill for everything I write as me.
  • One design skill per brand: Run Your City, AI@GSB, Out of Our Depth.
  • Helpers on top: winning-writing (Glenn's class as a skill), humanizer, interviewer, class prep.
10

Every skill gets good the same way

Key takeaway: Every skill gets good through the same loop: ask for 3, pick, name what's off, fix the skill, run it again.

  • A real round from my site: I asked for three bolder directions and picked B, the track lanes.
  • What was off: the start section was confusing and a thin line sat over the highlighter. Both went into my design skill.
  • Run it again: cleaner, still me.
  • A fix on the page lasts for that page. A fix in the skill carries into everything after.

Samples + tone + nevers = your voice

Key takeaway: Your voice skill is three things: samples, tone and nevers.

  • Samples are about you. Three real things you wrote teach more than a description of your style.
  • Tone is about the reader. For Run Your City I write for kids, so I keep the words simple.
  • Nevers are what you'd cringe at. Mine: "passionate about," em dashes, the little dots.
  • Next slide: what that looks like inside my actual file.
13

Inside my voice skill: just a text file

Key takeaway: A skill is just a text file. A short label on top, then your samples, tone and nevers in plain words.

  • This is the real file, trimmed. Is this a normal structure? Yes. The only required part is the top: a name and a description. Everything under it is whatever you want Claude to know.
  • 1, the frontmatter: Claude reads only this until something you ask matches it. That's why the description says "use whenever drafting anything Mary sends as herself."
  • 2, samples: it's built from my real emails, Slack and texts, down to phrases like "thank you so big."
  • 3, tone: a register map, one row per audience. Cold outreach is calm. The RYC team gets cheerleader mode.
  • 4, nevers: no em dashes, no "I hope this email finds you well," no "Regards."
  • Mine is 483 lines because I kept fixing it. Yours tonight can start at 30.
14

AI only knows what's in front of it

Key takeaway: AI only knows what's in front of it. Skills put the right stuff in front of it at the right time.

  • Context window: Claude's working memory. Everything in the chat counts. When it gets crowded, older stuff gets fuzzy and it drifts, so start a fresh chat. Your skills come with you.
  • Few-shot prompting: show, don't tell. Three real samples teach your style better than 30 rules. Same for design: show it two sites you love.
  • Progressive disclosure: Claude always sees every skill's name and description, opens the full file only when your ask matches, and pulls bundled files like a color sheet only if it needs them.
  • So the description is the most important line you'll write. Write it like a trigger: "use whenever I write as me."

Assets + vibe + nevers = your look

Key takeaway: A design skill is the same recipe as voice: assets, vibe, nevers.

  • Run Your City assets: real kids in every photo, all from our own homepage and programs, and the logo as is.
  • Vibe: pink, blue and navy, a bold headline font, warm and full of energy.
  • Nevers: stock photos, rainbows, black body text, thin icons, a stretched logo, kids dimmed behind text.
  • Don't know your taste? Give Claude two sites you love and ask what they share.

Ask for three. Name what's off.

Key takeaway: Building is the loop in action. Two skills plus one line, then fix the skill, not the page.

  • Hand over two skills plus one line: "Make me a personal website so people feel like they know me." Add your goal.

Six habits that save you an hour

Key takeaway: Six habits that keep AI sounding like you after tonight.
