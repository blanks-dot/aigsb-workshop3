07

Left alone, AI drifts to the average

  • Researchers played telephone with AI. One model draws a picture from a sentence, another describes the picture in words, and they repeat that 100 times.
  • 100 different starting sentences, run 700 times, ended up as just 12 kinds of picture: stormy lighthouses, cities at night, palace interiors.
  • Each description drops what's unusual, and each redraw fills the gap with the most common version.
  • They called the result visual elevator music. Your bio and your site drift the same way unless you steer. (Hintze et al., Patterns, 2025)
08

People trust work less when they think AI made it

  • 13 experiments, 5,000+ people. The work was identical. The only change was whether people were told AI helped.
  • Trust dropped in all 13. In these three it fell 16 to 20%: students judging a professor, investors judging an investment fund, clients judging a designer. (Schilke and Reimann, OBHDP, 2025)
  • Hiding AI isn't the answer. Make the output clearly yours.

Teach it once, sound & look like you everywhere

10

A skill is a recipe AI reads before it cooks

  • A skill is a recipe card. Claude can cook, it just doesn't know your family's recipe.
  • One voice skill for everything I write as me.
  • One design skill per brand: Run Your City, AI@GSB, Out of Our Depth.
  • Helpers on top: winning-writing (Glenn's class as a skill), humanizer, interviewer, class prep.
  • All of them got good through the same loop.
11

Every skill gets good the same way

  • Every skill improves the same way: ask for 3, pick your fav, name what's off, fix the skill, run it again.
  • A real round from my site: I asked for three bolder directions and picked B, the track lanes.
  • What was off: the start section was confusing and a thin line sat over the highlighter. Both went into my design skill.
  • Run it again: cleaner, still me. You'll run this loop on your voice, your design and your site.
  • A fix on the page lasts for that page. A fix in the skill carries into everything after.
12

Samples + tone + nevers = your voice

  • Samples are about you. Three real things you wrote teach more than a description of your style.
  • Tone is about the reader. For Run Your City I write for kids, so I keep the words simple.
  • Nevers are what you'd cringe at. Mine: "passionate about," em dashes, the little dots.
  • Decide who reads your site before you build. One skill can hold more than one mode.
13

Under the hood... let's get technical

  • Claude only reads a skill's name and description until your request matches it, so the title matters most.
  • Few-shot: a few real examples teach your style better than a list of rules.
  • The chat is the counter. Long chats get crowded and drift, so start fresh when it gets messy.
  • Write the description like a trigger, e.g. "use whenever I say cleanup."
14

Assets + vibe + nevers = your look

  • Same three parts as voice: assets, vibe, nevers. A brand guide is basically a design skill.
  • Run Your City assets: real kids in every photo, all from our own homepage and programs, and the logo as is.
  • Vibe: pink, blue and navy, a bold headline font, warm and full of energy.
  • Nevers: stock photos, rainbows, black body text, thin icons, a stretched logo, kids dimmed behind text.
  • If you don't know your taste, give Claude two sites you love and ask what they share.

Run Your City, one year apart

Not to make it about me, but here's mine

17

Ask for three. Name what's off.

  • You hand over two skills plus one line: "Make me a personal website so people feel like they know me." Add your goal.
  • Ask for 3, pick one, say why. I picked the marked-up page because it felt most like me.
  • Name what's off in plain words. Big-number stats felt corporate, tape on photos looked tacky. Claude swapped in plain bullets and a blue-pen outline.
  • Put the fix in the skill. Both became nevers, so they never come back.

Six habits that save you an hour

  • Start a new chat when it gets messy. Skills carry over.
  • Talk instead of type. Wispr Flow has a free plan.
  • Tell it who's reading before it writes.
  • Show a screenshot instead of describing.
  • Fix the skill, not the page.
  • Ask for 3 questions and a plan before it builds.
