Left alone, AI drifts to the average

Hintze et al., 2025

Why? Each round drops what's unusual and fills the gap with what's most common. 100 rounds = visual elevator music

- Researchers played telephone with AI. One model draws a picture from a sentence, another describes the picture in words, and they repeat that 100 times. - 100 different starting sentences, run 700 times, ended up as just 12 kinds of picture: stormy lighthouses, cities at night, palace interiors. - Each description drops what's unusual, and each redraw fills the gap with the most common version. - They called the result visual elevator music. Your bio and your site drift the same way unless you steer. (Hintze et al., Patterns, 2025)

People trust work less when they think AI made it

16 to 20%

lower trust once people learn AI was used

SCHILKE & REIMANN 2025  /  PROFESSORS, INVESTMENT FUNDS, DESIGNERS

- 13 experiments, 5,000+ people. The work was identical. The only change was whether people were told AI helped. - Trust dropped in all 13. In these three it fell 16 to 20%: students judging a professor, investors judging an investment fund, clients judging a designer. (Schilke and Reimann, OBHDP, 2025) - Hiding AI isn't the answer. Make the output clearly yours.
