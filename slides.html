Now the first skill: your voice. The formula: samples plus qualities plus nevers. Samples are the most important part. "Claude learns your voice the way a cover band learns a song: by listening to the recording, not by reading a description of it. Three real things you wrote do more than a paragraph about how you write." An email, a post, a text. Tip: you can talk instead of type. Hit the mic and just ramble about yourself. Qualities: "Which version of you shows up? You don't sound the same in a cover letter and a group chat." Pick Polished, Warm or Playful as a starting point. You can keep more than one inside the same voice skill and say "use my interview voice" later. Nevers: "The words that make you cringe when you see them. For me it's 'passionate about.' Write down three." Then test it. "Ask Claude for a two-line intro. Read it out loud. If a friend would say 'that's so you,' you're done. If not, add a better sample, not more rules."

Second skill: your look. Same shape as voice. Metaphor: "Think about briefing a florist for a wedding. You show them a Pinterest board (assets), you describe the mood (vibe), and you tell them what you hate: 'no carnations' (nevers). Three things, and they get it." Assets: what goes on the page, like a photo of you or your projects, and sites you love that set the bar. "You don't have to know design words. Pointing at a site you love is enough." And three words for how it should feel. Nevers: the icks. "Stock photos of people shaking hands. Rainbow gradients. The same three boxes every AI site has." Write down whatever makes you wince. Shortcut: "If you have no idea what you like, show Claude two sites you love and ask what they have in common. It'll name it for you."

One minute, right before they start. "Six habits I wish someone told me on day one." 1. New chat when it gets messy. "Claude reads the whole chat every time you send a message. After a long back and forth it gets foggy, and on a free plan it burns through your limit faster. Think of a whiteboard that's full: wipe it and start fresh. Your skills come with you, that's the whole point of them." 2. Talk, don't type. "Hit the mic and ramble. You'll say more, and it sounds more like you than what you'd type." 3. Let it interview you. "Before it writes anything, tell it: ask me five questions first. You get something about you, not about everyone." 4. Show, don't describe. "You don't need design words. A screenshot of a site you love says more than 'clean and modern.'" 5. Fix the skill, not the page. "If you're making the same correction twice, tell Claude: add this to my skill. Then it's fixed for every email and every page, forever." 6. Call your skill by name. "Claude usually picks the right skill on its own. If it doesn't, just say 'use my voice skill.'"
